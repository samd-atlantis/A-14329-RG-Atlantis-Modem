A-14329-MB00 modem power addendum - 2026-10-29

Decision: measure the first board as assembled. Do not automatically add 470 uF
to all boards. No bulk-capacitor or inductor ECO is approved by this desk review.
The ordered EC25AFFA-MINIPCIE remains on a power-validation hold. The carrier can
be brought up without the modem while the firmware and power tests proceed.

AS-ASSEMBLED EVIDENCE
The frozen assembly49 BOM and native records identify:
  PS1  IRM-20-5, 5 V / 4 A / 20 W, C6280121.
  U3   TPS62132RGTR fixed 3.3 V / 3 A; FSW pin 7 is grounded (high frequency).
  L2   SWPA5040S2R2NT, 2.2 uH, C96923; native attributes say +/-30%, 5.5 A Isat.
  C22  3.3 nF soft-start, 0603, C302028.
  C23  22 uF 0805, C77074; C24/C25 47 uF each 1210, C77101.
The nominal bulk total is 116 uF, with additional small bypasses. Effective MLCC
capacitance must be obtained from the fitted capacitor curves or measurement.
U2 is a separate TPS62132 for the router; neither buck's 3 A rating is its load.
The downstream 120 VAC controller terminals bypass the 20 W supply.

CONTROL-LOOP REVIEW
TI Table 9-2 checks 2.2 uH with nominal ceramic 10/22/47/100/200 uF, not 400 uF.
It permits 1 uH at high frequency. Equation 10 gives tSS = 1.25 V * CSS / 2.5 uA.
Consequently 3.3 nF gives about 1.65 ms and 22 nF about 11 ms. This is a nominal
estimate, not a measured startup time. Changing soft start does not establish
loop stability. Source [1], printed pages 14-16.

The fuller TI application-note matrix used 12 V input, 3.3 V output, 1 A load and
high frequency, with effective ceramic capacitance estimated at half nominal.
For 2.2 uH it marks 200 uF effective stable but 400/800 uF effective unstable.
For 1 uH it marks 400 uF effective stable outside its recommended range, and
800 uF effective unstable. Those are test points, not a guarantee between them
or for this board's 5 V input and mixed capacitor technology. Source [2], pages 1-2.

An illustrative 470 uF electrolytic plus 58 uF effective existing ceramics totals
528 uF effective. Electrolytics must not be given the ceramic 50% DC-bias factor.
With 2.2 uH, this is near published unstable test cases. Different ESR can change
the result, so this is a concrete reason for measurement, not proof it will fail.

The Quectel Mini PCIe guide's >=470 uF low-ESR recommendation and >=2.7 A supply
remain relevant to the card interface. AF's typical LTE-current table does not
provide a replacement transient maximum or explicit reduced-capacitance waiver.
The bare LGA EC25's 100 uF recommendation is for a different interface. See [4]
and the main electrical-audit.txt. This review found no justified paper-only waiver.

BOUNDED CONTINGENCY IF THE STOCK RAIL DROOPS
Candidate P1, on one engineering board only: L2 1.0 uH (+/-20% or better), add a
470 uF low-ESR bulk capacitor between 3V3_MODEM and GND_ISO near J8, and change
C22 to 22 nF. Retain the existing ceramics and FSW-low configuration. This is a
test candidate, NOT a verified or production-approved component substitution.

At an assumed 5.25 V input, 3.3 V output, 0.8 uH effective L, and typical 2.5 MHz,
the calculated inductor ripple is 0.613 A peak-to-peak. Peak is 3.006 A at 2.7 A
load, or 3.306 A at 3 A load. A 20% allowance at 3 A calls for at least 3.97 A
saturation capability: use >=4 A as a screening floor, plus verified inductance
versus current, RMS heating, DCR, dimensions and actual frequency. These are
conditional calculations, not worst-case current-limit or thermal qualification.
The TI minimum current-limit test is specified at 12 V; do not call this a
guaranteed 5 V limit margin. A lower actual switching frequency increases ripple.

For 586 uF nominal total, estimated capacitor-only startup current is 1.172 A
with 3.3 nF SS, versus 0.176 A with 22 nF SS. Add modem startup load to those
figures. The longer ramp reduces charging demand but can still alter modem
startup behavior. Confirm both startup and shutdown before reconnecting the card.

No exact replacement inductor or capacitor SKU is released: mixed-bank stability,
available mechanical space, useful ESR and actual current margin remain unknown.
The 1 uH candidate has more promising adjacent TI evidence than retaining 2.2 uH,
but neither is validated at this effective capacitance. Do not reduce L to
0.47 uH merely to match another application-note row; that is outside the data
sheet's stated minimum recommendation and increases current ripple. If P1 fails,
stop and select a supply/filter combination with a supported large-capacitance
design; do not keep adding capacitors or increase the nominal modem voltage.

FIRST-BOARD TEST PLAN
1. Leave the modem unplugged. Inspect fitted PSU, buck, L2, capacitor values and
   polarity. Record board serial, assembly files and baseline photographs.
   Use a qualified isolated power setup. A 5 V bench feed requires the AC input
   disconnected and PS1 output isolated or its backfeed behavior verified first.
2. At a suitable J8 load fixture, measure 3V3_MODEM and 5V_ISO simultaneously with
   short probe grounds. Record startup, shutdown and repeated MODEM_OFF cycles.
   Use load steps between approximately 0.1 A and 2.7 A with several pulse widths
   and edge rates, including a fast edge on the order of 10 us. Check light-load
   behavior, sustained load, and recovery when the load is removed. Capture the
   actual edge, voltage extrema, settling time and any repeated ringing. Repeat
   with the router busy and near the measured minimum/maximum 5 V input.
3. After startup, require the card-side rail to remain within 3.0-3.6 V and avoid
   startup overshoot above 3.6 V. Stop on sustained oscillation, restart/hiccup,
   excessive heating or unexplained current limiting. Use engineering margin
   inside the absolute operating window; an edge that only barely passes is not
   evidence of robust production performance. Scope load-release overshoot too.
4. Only after the unloaded/fixture checks pass, fit the EC25-AF, SIM and correct
   antennas. Capture attach, reconnect, sustained LTE upload, simultaneous Wi-Fi
   and four Ethernet links, and modem power cycles. Avoid a weak-signal test
   method that disconnects the transmitting antenna. Use controlled attenuation
   or the intended installation and record radio signal/power conditions.
5. If baseline fails, preserve its captures and try P1 on that one board. Begin
   without the modem and repeat steps 2-4. Verify the new capacitor's polarity,
   shortest practical power loop, insulated mechanical support and enclosure
   clearance. No unsupported loose capacitor installation is a production fix.
6. For either passing candidate, test inside the closed Integra enclosure through
   the intended temperature range and intended 120 VAC tolerance. Record local
   PSU ambient and regulator/inductor temperatures. Obtain stability evidence
   across load/temperature, ideally a measured loop response, before releasing an
   ECO. A single successful LTE session or a nominal-value simulation is insufficient.

5 V POWER HEADROOM
Source [3] rates PS1 at 20 W, 4 A. At 120 VAC its line-voltage curve allows full
output. The 5 V thermal curve begins derating above 45 C LOCAL ambient; approximate
readings are 18 W at 50 C, 14 W at 60 C and 10 W at 70 C, horizontal mounting.
Local ambient is the air around the supply inside the enclosure, not room air.

Engineering budget, not measurement: reserve 1 A at 3.3 V for the router; use
2.7 A at 3.3 V for the modem test; assume 85% buck efficiency and 0.5 W extra
5 V-side allowance. Required 5 V output is then 14.865 W, or 2.973 A. This leaves
5.135 W (25.7%) against the cool 20 W rating, but exceeds the 60 C derated rating.
At a full 3 A modem load the same budget is 16.029 W (3.206 A), leaving 19.9%
cool margin. At 50 C the respective margins fall to 17.4% and 10.9% of 18 W.
If both bucks actually delivered 3 A, this assumption would require 23.794 W:
the supply is not sized for both regulators' full rated outputs simultaneously.

Thus the nameplate supply is plausible for the expected loads, but a hot sealed
enclosure remains a real qualification item. The 85% conversion efficiency and
0.5 W allowance are explicit design assumptions, not vendor worst-case promises.
Measure PS1 output power and temperature; do not confuse its AC efficiency with
the two bucks' efficiencies or subtract the 120 VAC pass-through feed from 20 W.
